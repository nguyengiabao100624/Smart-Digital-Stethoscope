Abstract —Stethoscopes are a vital diagnostic tool in the med-
ical field. The amplification and recording capabilities of a
traditional stethoscope are constrained. Electronic stethoscopes
have been created to address this issue. Nowadays, doctors
frequently use conventional stethoscopes since they are less
expensive than electronic ones. In this study, a low-cost electronic
stethoscope with a hearing output jack and real-time cardiac
sound realization based on the Arduino UNO R3 is presented.
To enable the connection between the stethoscope and the laptop,
a Bluetooth module was used. With Excel, the heart sound
signal can be stored and displayed. It also activates additional
processing to determine heart rate.
Index Terms —electronic stethoscope, heart sound, PCG, aus-
cultation, Arduino
I. I NTRODUCTION
In 2019, according to the World Health Organization
(WHO), cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) were the main root
of death, with 17.9 million cases globally (32% of total
fatalities). In addition, about 75% of CVD-related deaths
are observed in countries with low and moderate incomes
[1]. For cardiovascular disease, early detection is essential
in starting treatment with counseling and medical care. One
of the fundamental techniques to diagnose heart disease is
employing a stethoscope to listen to the heartbeat, as known
as auscultation.
Understanding the features of the heart sound, known as
heart auscultation, is valuable data in diagnosing and monitor-
ing heart conditions such as heart murmurs, valve defects, and
irregular heartbeats. PCG stands for phonocardiography, a non-
invasive technique to detect and record the sounds produced
by the heart during each cardiac cycle [2].
Mechanical (Acoustic) Stethoscopes are the traditional
stethoscopes that have been used for decades. Conventional
stethoscopes have limitations in amplification and recording
capabilities. In auscultation learning, the instructor and medi-
cal student can not share the sound. It can not be applied in
remote healthcare monitoring and telemedicine consultations.
An electronic stethoscope is needed to cope with these issues.
Furthermore, a study demonstrated the effectiveness of the
digital stethoscope as a tool for teaching auscultatory skills
when compared to traditional methods [3].
Air-conduction and contact-conduction electronic stetho-
scopes are the two popular electronic stethoscopes, dependingon the sensor they employ. The electromagnetic coil or electret
capacitor is used as the sensor in the air-conduction electronic
stethoscope to gather sound signals with great stability and
strong reliability but limited sensitivity. Piezoelectric materi-
als, which have better anti-interference ability and sensitivity,
are primarily used as sound sensors in contact-conduction elec-
tronic stethoscopes. Unfortunately, any bending or deformation
to the piezoelectric components during the manufacturing
process reduces the stethoscope’s sensitivity [4].
Muhammad et al in [5] demonstrated an Arduino DUE-
based digital stethoscope with logging capabilities for post-
processing in Matlab and the ability to visualize heart sounds
in real-time through a graphic display. In the study on the
application of an electronic stethoscope, Oktivasari et al in [6]
designed a cost-effective and simple electronic stethoscope to
capture and measure real-time heart rate data (unit: BPM).
The results of the study show that this system does not
show a significant deviation from the conventional heart rate
measurement system, only about 2-5%, with a delay of 1.7 -
2 seconds.
In this study, we develop a low-cost electronic stethoscope
with heart sound visualization in a smart device via Bluetooth
and a 3.5mm hearing output for earphone connection.
II. M ETHODOLOGY
The block diagram of a proposed electronic stethoscope is
presented in Fig. 1. The stethoscope consists of three main
blocks: signal conditioning, analog front end (AFE), and signal
processing.
A. Signal Conditioning
The signal conditioning part contains a chest piece of the
conventional stethoscope to detect the mechanical sound wave
(sound of heartbeat) and a high-sensitive microphone attached
at the end of the chest piece.
The connected stem, diaphragm, and bell make up the
stethoscope’s chest piece. The chest piece’s smaller round end
is known as the bell half. One side of the gadget concentrates
on a smaller spectrum of lower-frequency sounds. The large
disk-shaped end of the chest piece is called the diaphragm,
it is a sealed membrane. This side of the device picks up a
higher-frequency sound than the bell half. The sound of the

The microphone functions as a transducer, which is made

signal with a proportional output. The selected microphone,

impedance of 2.2kΩ, and a frequency range of 20Hz−20kHz .
The microphone operates with a voltage range of 1−10V DC .
B. Analog-front-end (AFE)
The electrical signal converted by the microphone is then
amplified by the analog front end (AFE), consisting of two
stages. The gain of the first stage is configured as a fixed-
gain voltage. The capacitor Cmic= 10uFof the microphone
removes the DC level of the signal.(Fig. 3).
Before entering the second buffer stage, the signal passes
through capacitor C2to remove the DC offset from the first
stage. In the second stage, the volume of the sound can be
adjusted easily by tuning variable resistor R4. After being
amplified at the two stages, the signal goes through a passive
low-pass filter RC to filter out noise (at a frequency higher than

C. Signal processing
1) PCG signal study: A sample PCG signal is shown in
Fig. 4. Systole and diastole are the two phases in a heart cycle.
Diastole is the phase when the heart relaxes, while systole is
its contracting phase.
Fig. 4. Sample PCG signal [7].
Systole occurs when the heart valves close and blood
is discharged from the ventricles into the pulmonary and
systemic circulation. This produces the first heart sound, often
known as S1. Low-frequency S1 is most audible at the top
of the heart. The heart valves open to let blood flow into the
heart as the heart chambers fill with blood during diastole. The
second heart sound, sometimes referred to as S2, is created
when the atrioventricular valves open, causing blood to move

2) Transmission circuit: The obtained analog signal, after
going through the amplifier and filter, can be heard by ear-

is transmitted to the laptop to be stored in Excel files. Stored
data are plotted to calibrate the signal.

B. Heart sound measurement
To determine the phases and shape of the signal, the
measurements are set up with different steps. First, the chest
piece is put on the chest of the subject (a volunteer). Second,
the circuit is connected via Bluetooth to a laptop running the
Excel program. Once in operation, the circuit continuously
sends the output signals to the laptop for storage in Excel
files. Each of these files contains 10 seconds of the heart sound.
(Fig. 8) clearly confirms that the measured PCG signal has four
different phases: S1, systole, S2, and diastole, as expected. The
S1 peak is higher than the S2 peak.

To evaluate the heart rate counting algorithm, the heart rate
results of the proposed electronic stethoscope are compared
with commercially available pulse oximeters. The comparison
is taken on 5 different subjects of different health conditions
and genders, with ages ranging from 20 to 25 years old. We
calculated the average heart rate measured by each product,
then found the delta, which is determined as the difference
between two HR A VG values.
The heart rate results (compared to commercial devices) (see
table III) show that with a heart rate range from 75 to 85, our
design nearly fits the commercial device results (delta range
0.7 to 1.0). However, with a heart rate lower or higher than
that range, our system is not good. There are some reasons
below that make this happen:
•The measurement results are strongly impacted by the en-
vironment (low noise environment required) and motion
artifacts.
•The threshold level is fixed at a specific value while this
value, in turn, varies depending on some factors such as
health condition, gender, environment, or motion artifacts.
It is obvious that this area needs further improvement by
testing on a bigger group of subjects to identify the most
efficient threshold level

IV. C ONCLUSION
This study described the development of a real-time and
low-cost electronic stethoscope for the auscultation of heart
sounds. For use in medical diagnosis and auscultation learning,
the stethoscope produces clear sound recordings. The PCG wasused to determine the heart rate, and the results were compared
with a commercial device. The design will be improved in the
future by optimizing it to increase performance.
